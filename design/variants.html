Assay — design directions

Six visual systems applied to the same real artifact — the hazard decision card — so styles compete on identical content. Pick a direction; tokens for each are listed under its render. House law holds in every variant: no gauges, no confidence percentages, machine tokens in mono, one primary action per card.

1 · Monochrome, productionized current direction, refined

What the wireframes already are, given elevation, states and micro-contrast instead of flat hairlines. Signals restraint and seriousness; the one-ink-button system is the refusal brand — nothing decorates, so the single black action carries all the weight. Risk: reads austere on a marketing page; in-product that austerity is the point.

2 · Dark minimal the Linear school — dark-first, borders barely there

The default dialect of 2024–26 dev tooling (Linear, Vercel, Supabase): near-black tokens-first dark mode, hierarchy from weight and spacing, colour reserved for state. Signals "built for people who live in this tool"; fits Assay's engineer audience and makes the held/ink states glow. Risk: fifty-percent-of-new-SaaS sameness — the voice, not the theme, must do the differentiating.

text ramp#EDEEF0 · #9FA1A7 · #64666Dshadownone — borders do the work
accentlight ink #E8E9EB (action only)fontsInter + Roboto Mono

3 · Polished fintech the Stripe school — trust through finish

Light, crisp, one saturated brand accent, soft layered shadows, generous radii: the grammar of products that touch money. Signals institutional trust — useful for a product whose pitch is "believe our data". Risk: the accent colour starts making promises; Assay's law says colour belongs to state, so the accent here stays on the single primary action and nowhere else.

accent#4F46E5 (primary action only)fontsInter + Roboto Mono

4 · Warm editorial the Notion school — paper, serif, calm

Warm paper background, serif display over humanist body, chrome that almost disappears. Signals "a thoughtful document, not a machine" — flattering to Assay's first-person prose voice, which is the product's strongest asset. Risk: softness can read as imprecision in a tool whose subject is whether a number is true; the mono tokens have to work harder to keep the machine's edges visible.

text ramp#37352F · #6B675F · #A5A199shadowhairline border, no shadow
accentwarm ink #37352FfontsLora (display) + Inter + Roboto Mono

5 · Dense enterprise the Datadog/Grafana school — density is respect

Compact type, tight spacing, visible structure, minimal radius: built for operators comparing many things at once. Signals "this is an instrument"; the right dialect if Assay's buyer runs hundreds of scrapers, and the run/fields tables would thrive in it. Risk: the decision card is a reading task, not a monitoring task — density fights the five-second decision.

bg / surface#F2F3F5 · #FFFFFFradius4 / 3
text ramp#1d1f24 · #565b66 · #8b909bshadow1px borders everywhere, no shadow
accentink #1D1F24 · 12px base typefontsInter (12px) + Roboto Mono

6 · Brutalist utilitarian the dev-tool zine — honesty as aesthetic

Flat white, 2px rules, zero radius, zero shadows, mono everywhere it can get away with it. Signals "nothing here is dressed up" — which is literally Assay's pitch, making this the most on-thesis and the most polarizing option. Risk: reads hobbyist to enterprise buyers; brutalism sells honesty to individuals and rawness to procurement.

accent#000 + 2px rulesfontsRoboto Mono (display) + Inter

Recommendation

Ship 1 (monochrome refined) as the light theme and 2 (dark minimal) as the dark theme off the same tokens — Assay's brand is refusal, and a system where the only saturated thing on screen is the one action you may take is that brand rendered as CSS. Variant 4's serif display is worth stealing for the landing page and incident record, where the first-person prose voice carries the argument; leave 3, 5 and 6 as documented roads-not-taken.
