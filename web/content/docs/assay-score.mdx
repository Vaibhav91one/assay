---
title: The Assay Score
description: The gate's decision as one word, the arithmetic behind each word, and why the product shows the word and not the number.
---

Wherever Assay held a cell or published a heal, it shows one word: **CLEAR**,
**AGREED**, **THIN**, **WEAK**, **GONE**, **POLICY** or **BRAKED**. That word is
the Assay Score.

It is not a rating and it is not a score out of anything. It names which branch
of one two-part test the run took. This page is the whole of that test, with the
measured numbers, so that a word on a screen can always be turned back into
arithmetic you can check.

---

## The gate

Every heal Assay publishes has to pass both halves of one condition:

```
score > tau (0.60)   AND   score − runner_up > delta (0.16)
```

`score` is the weighted similarity between the element Assay lost and the best
candidate it found on the changed page, in `[0, 1]`. `runner_up` is the same
number for the second-best candidate. The subtraction between them is the
**margin**.

**Both conditions, not either.** They guard different failures, and each one is
blind to the other's:

| | guards against | fails when |
|---|---|---|
| `tau` | *the element is gone.* Nothing on the page is a plausible replacement, so the best candidate is merely the least bad one | the page dropped the field, or redesigned it past recognition |
| `delta` | *two things look equally right.* Something is a plausible replacement — and so is something else | a decoy appeared: a second price, an archived copy of the headline, a duplicated card |

A candidate can look very much like the thing you lost and still be the wrong
answer, if something else looks nearly as much like it. When two candidates are
that close, picking one is a coin flip, and a coin flip is not a heal.

`delta` is the cheap one and the one nobody else has. Scrapling has a floor and
no margin; Healenium has a cap and no margin; COLOR suggests a fix for every
broken locator. Comparing the top two costs one subtraction.

### 0.60 and 0.16 are fitted, not chosen

`npm run sweep` fits them. It ranks each benchmark case once and then replays a
grid of threshold pairs over the stored ranking: 11 values of `tau` from 0.30 to
0.85, 10 values of `delta` from 0 to 0.40, **110 points**, each scored over all
153 cases. `results/sweep.json` holds the whole surface, and `0.60 / 0.16` is
the point it recommends.

They are therefore **arguments to every tool, not constants**. There is no
evidence they transfer to another vertical, another field type, or another
site's markup conventions — see
[Limitations §5](/docs/limitations#the-thresholds-are-calibrated-on-this-corpus-and-nowhere-else).
A field contract can set its own pair; `field_runs.ranked` keeps the scores the
run was actually judged on either way.

## The seven words

Five come from the gate itself. `healGated` in `src/heal.ts` returns exactly
five reasons and no others.

| Word | Reason code | What happened | Published? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CLEAR** | `clear_margin` | One candidate cleared `tau` and was more than `delta` ahead of the next. | yes |
| **AGREED** | `benign_tie` | The top two were within `delta` of each other — but they carried **the same text**, so it does not matter which node the value was read off. | yes |
| **THIN** | `thin_margin` | The top two were within `delta` and carried **different** values. A coin flip. | no |
| **WEAK** | `below_tau` | The best candidate on the page did not clear `tau`. Nothing looked enough like this field to be a candidate at all. | no |
| **GONE** | `no_candidates` | There was nothing to rank. The gate abstained before anything was weighed, so there is genuinely no score and no margin. | no |

Two more come from a **policy** overriding a heal the gate allowed. These are
the cases where the arithmetic said yes and something else said no, and they are
kept separate on purpose: an operator reading `THIN` on a field they themselves
set to `auto_approve: never` is being told the wrong thing about their own
configuration.

| Word | Reason code | What happened | Published? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **POLICY** | `auto_approve_floor:<n>` | The gate cleared it, and the field contract's `auto_approve` floor asks for a person above that score. | no — it is queued |
| **BRAKED** | `brake_engaged` | Healing is stopped on this field by an operator, or by the ping-pong detector. Nothing is attempted. | no |

The brake is checked **before** the floor. An operator saying "stop healing this
field" outranks an arithmetic threshold, and reporting the threshold when a
brake is what stopped it would send them to tune the wrong number.

<Callout type="warn" title="Some held cells show no word at all, and that is deliberate">
  Two cases produce no band, and in both the raw reason code is printed instead:

  **A reason this build does not know.** A code added to the engine can reach a
  screen whose table predates it. Rather than fall back to the nearest word,
  Assay shows nothing — a confident wrong word is worse than a code you can
  search for. `src/reports/vocabulary.ts` has drawn this line for reason wording
  since before the band existed.

  **`brake_unreadable:<message>`.** The brake table could not be *read*, so the
  run failed closed and withheld the heal. That is not the same fact as a brake
  being *set*: nobody may have stopped this field, and **BRAKED** would send an
  operator to clear a brake that does not exist while the real fault — an
  unreachable database — went unnamed.
</Callout>

<Callout title="A published heal currently shows the diff and no word">
  `src/runner.ts` writes `{ status: 'healed' }` with no reason, so
  `clear_margin` and `benign_tie` live on the proof record in
  `results/events.jsonl` and never reach the `field_runs.reason` column the band
  is read from. **CLEAR** and **AGREED** are defined here, and are what
  `assay_propose` reports over MCP, but the run screen draws the before/after
  selector diff for a heal and claims nothing about which of the two it was. It
  will, unchanged, the day that reason is persisted.
</Callout>

## What the gate is worth, measured

`npm run bench` mutates three archived recall pages — mattel, ikea and chicco,
six captures each, ten mutations — for **153 cases** with exact ground truth. It
runs three arms over the same cases:

| arm | n | exact | value OK | **value wrong** | abstained |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| naive (first tag match) | 153 | 31.4% | 31.4% | 60.8% | 7.8% |
| similarity, no gate | 153 | 72.5% | 76.5% | **23.5%** | 0.0% |
| margin gate (τ 0.60 / δ 0.16) | 153 | 60.8% | 64.7% | **0.0%** | 35.3% |

**Value wrong** is a confidently incorrect value returned as though it were
right. It is the number nobody in this field publishes and it is the whole point
of the project. The gate takes it from 23.5% to zero, and the price is 35.3%
abstentions — 54 cases of the 153, of which `results/sweep.json` counts 18 as
necessary and 36 as unnecessary.

That is the trade in full: **a hole instead of a lie, and about a third of the
holes did not need to be there.** Both halves are on this page because both are
true.

<Callout type="warn" title="Every abstention ever recorded came from a manufactured case">
  `npm run replay` runs the real archived drift — 74 runs across the three
  sites, two and a half years of it — and produces **8 clean runs, 66 heals and
  zero abstentions**. When those pages actually moved, the right answer won by a
  wide margin.

  So on the only real-world data in this repository the margin gate is inert: it
  costs nothing and it catches nothing. The claim "the gate prevents wrong
  values" rests on constructed cases and on no observed production incident. See
  [Limitations](/docs/limitations).
</Callout>

## Why a word and not a number

The obvious thing to build is a confidence percentage on every cell.
`docs/FEATURES.md` §4 refuses it, on three grounds, and the band was designed to
answer all three rather than to get around them.

**1. A float lets every downstream team pick its own threshold.** That relocates
the abstain decision to whoever cares least about it — someone filtering
`confidence > 0.8` in a warehouse query, months later, who has never seen this
page. There is nothing to re-threshold in a closed set of seven words. The
decision stays where the evidence is.

**2. `0.94` on everything trains people to ignore the column.** A number that is
always high is furniture. The band is not printed on every cell: it appears only
where the gate reached a decision worth reading, and four of the seven words
mean *this one is waiting on you*.

**3. The gate already made the decision; exporting the raw score asks the user
to make it again with less information.** The gate had the ranked list, the full
element text, the thresholds in force and the tie check. A reader given `0.7354`
has one of those four. The band reports the conclusion; this page holds the
evidence.

So what the product shows is a word, and what it shows beside the word is the
thing a person can actually act on. On a **THIN** refusal that is the two rival
selectors **and the values they carry** — a human can look at
`Recall & Safety Alerts` beside `Recall & Safety Alerts (archived)` and answer in
five seconds. The scores that failed to separate them cannot be judged that way,
which is why they are not there.

### What is still refused

The amendment to `docs/FEATURES.md` §4, dated 2026-08-23, overturns exactly one
thing — the band word — and nothing else:

- **No float, anywhere in the UI.** No percentage, no progress bar, no gauge, no
  ring, no score column, no per-cell confidence.
- **Nothing added to the export.** `_assay.fields[].status` still carries only
  the closed vocabulary `live` / `stale` / `healed` / `degraded` /
  `quarantined`. The REST envelope and the proof record are unchanged: the band
  is a rendering, not a field.
- **No band on a cell the gate did not decide.** A clean read is `live`, and
  that is all it is.

### The numbers did not go anywhere

They are in the store and they are in this repository. `field_runs.ranked` holds
every candidate the gate weighed with its score, written at abstain time and
never recomputed. `results/events.jsonl` holds one proof record per run with the
candidates, the scores, the runner-up, the margin and the thresholds in force.
`results/sweep.json` holds all 110 threshold pairs.

The band is derived from `field_runs.reason` and from nothing else — never
recomputed from the score and the thresholds. A band worked out by re-running
the arithmetic would be a second thing that can be wrong: it disagrees with the
row it is printed on the moment a contract is edited underneath it. One
decision, recorded once, rendered once.
