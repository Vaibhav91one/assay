Assay has a small vocabulary and uses it consistently. This page is the record of
which word means what, and — where a concept has both a screen word and a data
token — which one you are looking at.

<Callout title="Screen words and data tokens are different, and neither may be renamed for the other">
  The words in **What you read on a screen** are user-facing and can be improved.
  The words in `code font` are stored values — `field_runs.reason`, the
  `status` in a published envelope — and consumers branch on them. Renaming one
  of those is a schema change, and this page never asks for it.
</Callout>

---

## The three words for not publishing

These are the ones most often used as if they were interchangeable. They are
three different things: an action, a state, and a stored token.

### Abstain

**What the gate does.** When a candidate fails either threshold, the gate
abstains: it declines to publish. Abstaining is an *event* — it happens once,
during a run.

The benchmark counts abstentions (`54 of 153`), because that is the price side
of the [honest trade](/docs/#the-honest-trade).

### Held for review

**What the cell then is, and the phrase every screen uses.** The cell is not
empty and not wrong — it exists, it is known, and Assay is deliberately
withholding it until a person answers one question about it. It shows up on
**Decisions**.

<Callout title="This is the canonical user-facing phrase">
  A cell the gate refused is **held for review** — not "quarantined", not
  "abstained", not "blocked", not "pending". One phrase, everywhere a person
  reads it. Shorten it to **held** where the surrounding sentence already says
  what for; never lengthen it.
</Callout>

### `quarantined`

**The stored token for the same state.** It is the value of `status` in the
published envelope (`src/envelope.ts`), and it is what a downstream consumer
branches on:

```json
{
  "price": null,
  "_assay": { "fields": { "price": { "status": "quarantined", "reason": "thin_margin" } } }
}
```

`null` **plus** `status: "quarantined"` is the whole point. A bare `null` cannot
be told apart from a field that was genuinely empty, and the difference between
"this is empty" and "we would not swear to this" is the product.

The full closed set of statuses is `live`, `healed`, `quarantined`, `stale`,
`degraded`.

## The decision

### Heal

Re-finding a field after the page moved under it. Candidates on the new page are
ranked by weighted similarity against a stored fingerprint of what the field used
to look like, and the winner is a *proposed* heal — not yet a published one.

### Gate

The two tests a proposed heal has to pass before anything is published:

```
score > tau   AND   score - runner_up > delta
```

Both, or nothing is published and the cell is held for review. The second test is
the one that makes Assay different: a candidate can look very much like the thing
you lost and still be wrong, if something else looks nearly as much like it.

### τ (tau) — the **floor**

How much a candidate has to look like the field at all. Default `0.60`. Below it,
nothing on the page was a plausible candidate.

### δ (delta) — the **lead**

How far ahead of the runner-up the winner has to be. Default `0.16`. Below it,
two candidates were too close to separate and choosing between them is a coin
flip.

<Callout title="Settings says floor and lead, and that is the same fact">
  The Settings screen renders these as **"0.60 floor, 0.16 lead"** rather than as
  τ and δ. Same two numbers, same two tests. The Greek letters are the code's
  names and this page keeps them so the [assay-score page](/docs/assay-score) and
  `src/heal.ts` can be read against each other; the screens use the English.
</Callout>

### Tier

A named pair of thresholds a [contract](#contract) can put on a field, so nobody
hand-tunes deltas per field:

| Tier | floor (τ) | lead (δ) | On the 135-case corpus |
|---|---|---|---|
| `strict` | 0.70 | 0.20 | 84 correct, 0 wrong, 37.8% abstained |
| `normal` (default) | 0.60 | 0.16 | 93 correct, 0 wrong, 31.1% abstained |
| `loose` | 0.60 | 0.12 | 105 correct, **6 wrong**, 17.8% abstained |

`loose` forfeits the zero-wrong-values claim for that field. That is stated in
numbers because it is the reason the tier is opt-in.

## The bands

One word for the gate's decision, read off the stored reason and never
recomputed. Seven of them, and they are a closed set — see
[The Assay Score](/docs/assay-score) for the arithmetic behind each.

| Band | Stored reason | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **CLEAR** | `clear_margin` | One candidate matched and was well clear of everything else. |
| **AGREED** | `benign_tie` | The best candidates were too close to separate, but they all carried the **same value**, so which one it was read from does not matter. |
| **THIN** | `thin_margin` | Too close to separate **and** carrying different values. Held. |
| **WEAK** | `below_tau` | Nothing on the page matched this field well enough to be a candidate. Held. |
| **GONE** | `no_candidates` | There was nothing on the page to compare against at all. Held. |
| **POLICY** | `auto_approve_floor:<n>` | The gate would have published this; the contract asks a person to look first. |
| **BRAKED** | `brake_engaged` | Healing is stopped on this field by an operator. Held. |

A reason with no band draws **nothing**. That is a real answer and not a bug: an
unknown reason code, or `brake_unreadable:<message>` — which is deliberately
*not* BRAKED, because a brake that could not be read is not a brake that is set.

### Why the reason codes read the way they do

`src/reports/vocabulary.ts` turns the stored code into a sentence a person can
act on. Three of them, verbatim:

| Code | What a person is shown |
|---|---|
| `thin_margin` | two candidates on the page were too close to call |
| `below_tau` | nothing on the page looked enough like this field to be a candidate |
| `no_candidates` | the element is gone and nothing took its place |

A code with **no** wording in that table is printed **as a code**, marked
untranslated. It is never given an invented adjective — a record whose whole
value is that it does not fabricate cannot start by fabricating a word.

`benign_tie` has no entry there because it is not a refusal: it is the gate
publishing after finding the tie did not matter.

## The scraper

### Contract

The per-target YAML that says which fields exist and what policy each is gated
at — [tier](#tier), or explicit τ and δ, what to do on abstain, and where to
alert. Versions are appended and never edited, so "what did the contract say when
that cell was published" is always answerable. A field the contract does not
mention is gated at the engine defaults, and that is stated rather than left
blank.

### Cadence

How often a target runs. `hourly`, `daily`, `weekly`, or a count like `6h` or
`2d`. Default `6h`. `paused` is **not** a cadence — pausing is an operation, and
a paused target keeps the cadence it will resume on.

### Anchors

The landmark elements around a field that Assay records alongside it — the things
that let it say *where* the field was, not just what it looked like. When several
independent anchors stop agreeing with each other, that is semantic drift, and it
is a break signal even though nothing errored.

### Skeleton

A hash of the page's structure with **all text excluded** — that exclusion is
what makes it a skeleton rather than a page hash. A changed skeleton is recorded
as context, never as a break on its own: sites redesign constantly without
breaking anything.

## The record

### Episode

One break, from the run that detected it to the run where the field recovered. It
is the unit of alerting: a second break on the same field inside an open episode
is the *same* incident, which is how a template change across 400 pages sends one
message with a count instead of 400 messages.

### Proof record

The `_assay` block attached to every published row: the run, the proof id, and
per field the status and the reason. Behind a *show the numbers* disclosure it
also carries the candidates, their scores, the margin and the thresholds in force
at the time.

### Proof id

`pr_9f21c4…` — the handle on one published cell. It is what
[`/api/v1/explain/:proof`](/docs/api-reference) and `assay explain` take, and it
resolves to the full provenance for that cell. It is stable, which is why a
target with run history can be paused but never deleted: deleting the runs would
break the proof id on every row already published from it.
